Anaphylaxis

Airway, breathing or circulation involvement after an exposure (food, drug, sting).

  1. Epinephrine 0.3–0.5 mg IM (1 mg/mL) q5–15 min PRN. Pediatrics: 0.01 mg/kg IM (1 mg/mL), max 0.5 mg. Give first, and don’t delay.
  2. NaCl 0.9% (Normal Saline) Hypotension: bolus 500–1,000 mL IV, reassess. Pediatrics: 20 mL/kg bolus.
  3. Salbutamol (Albuterol/Ventolin) Bronchospasm despite epinephrine: nebulized 2.5–5 mg q20 min PRN x3. Don’t rely on salbutamol alone.
  4. Diphenhydramine (Benadryl) Adjunct for itch/urticaria: 25–50 mg PO/IV/IM q4–6h PRN. Not a substitute for epinephrine.
  5. Epinephrine Refractory shock: infusion 2–10 mcg/min IV, titrate to perfusion/BP (physician).

Escalation & notes: Observe for recurrence (biphasic reaction) per protocol. Refractory reaction → physician and medevac planning.

Cardiac arrest — VF / pulseless VT

Shockable rhythm. High-quality CPR and defibrillation come first; drugs are adjuncts.

  1. Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO (0.1 mg/mL syringe) q3–5 min.
  2. Amiodarone 300 mg IV/IO push; may repeat 150 mg once.
  3. Lidocaine (IV Antiarrhythmic) Alternative: 1–1.5 mg/kg IV/IO push; may repeat 0.5–0.75 mg/kg q5–10 min (max cumulative 3 mg/kg).
  4. Magnesium Sulphate Torsades: 2 g IV over 10–15 min.
  5. Calcium gluconate Suspected hyperkalemia: 1–2 g IV (see Hyperkalemia).
  6. Sodium Bicarbonate TCA overdose with wide QRS: 1–2 mmol/kg IV bolus.

Escalation & notes: Look for reversible causes: hypoxia, hypovolemia, hyper-/hypokalemia, hypothermia, toxins, tamponade, tension pneumothorax, thrombosis.

Symptomatic bradycardia

Slow rate with hypotension, altered mental status, chest pain or heart failure.

  1. Atropine Sulfate 1 mg IV push q3–5 min PRN (max 3 mg).
  2. Epinephrine Atropine ineffective: infusion 2–10 mcg/min IV, titrate to perfusion/BP (physician).
  3. Dopamine Alternative: start 5 mcg/kg/min IV; titrate by 2.5–5 mcg/kg/min q5–10 min (typical range 2–20 mcg/kg/min).

Escalation & notes: Transcutaneous pacing if available and trained. Look for causes: ischemia, drugs (beta-blockers), hyperkalemia, hypothermia.

Narrow-complex tachycardia (SVT)

Regular narrow-complex tachycardia in a stable patient.

  1. Vagal manoeuvres first.
  2. Adenosine 6 mg rapid IV push + immediate 20 mL flush; if no conversion, 12 mg; may repeat 12 mg once. Pediatrics: 0.1 mg/kg (max 6 mg), then 0.2 mg/kg (max 12 mg).
  3. Metoprolol Rate control (e.g., AF/flutter) with physician: 2.5–5 mg slow IV over 2 min; repeat q5 min to max 15 mg if BP/HR tolerate.

Escalation & notes: Unstable (hypotension, altered mental status, chest pain) → synchronized cardioversion per protocol/physician.

Seizure / status epilepticus

Seizure lasting more than 5 minutes, or repeated seizures without recovery.

  1. Dextrose (D50W) Check glucose. If low: 25 g IV.
  2. Midazolam (Versed) No IV access: 5–10 mg IM or IN (protocol dependent).
  3. Lorazepam (Ativan) IV access: 0.1 mg/kg IV (max 4 mg per dose); may repeat once after 5–10 min.
  4. Diazepam (Valium) Alternative IV: 5–10 mg IV slow push; may repeat q5–10 min PRN.
  5. Phenytoin (Dilantin) Still seizing after benzodiazepines: 15–20 mg/kg IV at ≤50 mg/min with ECG/BP monitoring (normal saline only).
  6. Thiamine (B1) Alcohol use or malnutrition: 100 mg IV/IM.
  7. Magnesium Sulphate Eclampsia: 4–6 g IV load, then 1–2 g/h (physician/OB protocol).

Escalation & notes: Protect the airway and keep the patient from injury on a moving deck. Call the physician for any repeat dosing.

Opioid toxicity

Reduced level of consciousness with slow or shallow breathing and small pupils.

  1. Ventilate first (bag-valve-mask with oxygen).
  2. Naloxone (Narcan) Titrate: 0.04 mg IV if opioid-dependent, escalating 0.04 → 0.4 → 2 mg IV/IM q2–3 min until breathing improves. Intranasal: 4 mg; repeat q2–3 min PRN. Pediatrics: 0.1 mg/kg (max 2 mg per dose).

Escalation & notes: Naloxone wears off before many opioids. Monitor for re-sedation; long-acting opioids may need repeat doses or an infusion (physician).

Hypoglycemia

Low glucose with symptoms, especially confusion or reduced level of consciousness.

  1. Awake and able to swallow: oral glucose/carbohydrates.
  2. Dextrose (D50W) 25 g IV (one amp D50W); recheck glucose in 10–15 min; repeat PRN. Pediatrics: D10W 5 mL/kg.
  3. Glucagon No IV access: 1 mg IM/subcut (≥25 kg); 0.5 mg IM/subcut (<25 kg).
  4. Thiamine (B1) Malnutrition/alcohol: give alongside glucose, but don’t delay dextrose.

Escalation & notes: After recovery: oral carbohydrates, find the cause (insulin, sulfonylurea, alcohol), and plan monitoring.

Hyperkalemia

High potassium, especially with ECG changes.

  1. Calcium gluconate ECG changes: 1–2 g IV over 5–10 min; may repeat if ECG changes persist.
  2. Insulin Human Regular Shift: 10 units regular insulin IV plus dextrose; recheck glucose (delayed hypoglycemia).
  3. Dextrose (D50W) With insulin: 25 g IV unless glucose is markedly elevated.
  4. Salbutamol (Albuterol/Ventolin) Adjunct: high-dose nebulized 10–20 mg per protocol.
  5. Sodium Bicarbonate Adjunct only in severe acidemia. Not definitive therapy.

Escalation & notes: These only protect the heart and move potassium into cells. Removal isn’t available at sea, so plan medevac early.

Acute coronary syndrome / STEMI

Ischemic chest pain; STEMI on ECG. PCI is not available at sea.

  1. ASA (Aspirin) 160–325 mg PO, chewed ASAP.
  2. Nitroglycerin spray 0.4 mg SL q5 min up to 3 doses if SBP ≥90–100 mmHg. Ask about PDE5 inhibitors first.
  3. Clopidogrel Load 300 mg (up to 600 mg per pathway), then 75 mg PO daily.
  4. Tenecteplase STEMI without timely PCI: weight-based single IV bolus (physician-only, with checklist). <60 kg: 30 mg; 60–69 kg: 35 mg; 70–79 kg: 40 mg; 80–89 kg: 45 mg; ≥90 kg: 50 mg.
  5. Enoxaparin With lysis, <75 years: 30 mg IV then 1 mg/kg subcut q12h (max 100 mg first 2 doses). ≥75 years: 0.75 mg/kg q12h (max 75 mg first 2 doses), no IV bolus.
  6. Fentanyl Ongoing pain: 25–50 mcg IV slow push; repeat q5–10 min PRN.

Escalation & notes: Get the 12-lead and the shore physician involved early, and plan medevac at the same time as treatment.

Severe asthma exacerbation

Increased work of breathing, inability to speak in sentences, falling SpO₂.

  1. Salbutamol (Albuterol/Ventolin) Nebulized 2.5–5 mg q20 min PRN x3, or MDI 4–8 puffs via spacer q20 min x3; then reassess.
  2. Prednisone 40–50 mg PO daily for 5 days.
  3. Dexamethasone Alternative: 6–10 mg PO/IV once daily for 1–2 days (local protocols vary).
  4. Magnesium Sulphate Not responding to bronchodilators/steroids: 2 g IV over 15–30 min.
  5. Epinephrine If anaphylaxis is the cause: IM epinephrine (see Anaphylaxis).

Escalation & notes: Silent chest, exhaustion, confusion or falling SpO₂ → physician and medevac.

Major hemorrhage / trauma

Significant bleeding or major trauma (machinery, falls, crush injury).

  1. Control bleeding first: direct pressure, packing, tourniquet.
  2. Tranexamic Acid 1 g IV over 10 min, then 1 g IV over 8 h. Start within 3 h of injury. Pediatrics: 15 mg/kg IV (max 1 g).
  3. Lactated Ringer’s Bolus 500–1,000 mL IV, then reassess; avoid fluid overload.
  4. Fentanyl Analgesia: 25–50 mcg IV slow push; repeat q5–10 min PRN (or 0.5–1 mcg/kg).
  5. Ketamine Analgesia alternative: 0.1–0.3 mg/kg IV slow over ~10–15 min.

Escalation & notes: Keep the casualty warm, reassess often, and request medevac early.

Smoke inhalation & cyanide

Casualty from an enclosed-space fire. Think carbon monoxide and cyanide.

  1. High-flow 100% oxygen.
  2. Cyanokit (Hydroxocobalamin) Altered consciousness, hypotension, seizures or high lactate: 5 g IV over ~15 min; may repeat 5 g once. Pediatrics: 70 mg/kg (max 5 g).
  3. Sodium Thiosulfate Adjunct: 12.5 g IV over ~10 min (physician/toxicology).
  4. Salbutamol (Albuterol/Ventolin) Bronchospasm: nebulized 2.5–5 mg.
  5. Silver Sulfadiazine 1% Cream Burns: after cooling and assessment. See the entry for use.

Escalation & notes: Airway burns (soot, hoarse voice, stridor) can worsen quickly. Involve the physician early and plan medevac.

Alcohol withdrawal after sailing

Tremor, sweating, anxiety or agitation 1–3 days after leaving port; can progress to seizures.

  1. Thiamine (B1) 100 mg IV/IM daily. High-dose IV for suspected Wernicke’s (physician).
  2. Diazepam (Valium) Symptom-triggered dosing per protocol; avoid over-sedation.
  3. Lorazepam (Ativan) Consider when hepatic impairment is suspected (physician-directed).

Escalation & notes: Check glucose and hydration. Seizures → see Seizure. Ask about intake before sailing and after port visits.
